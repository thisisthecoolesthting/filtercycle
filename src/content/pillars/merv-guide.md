## MERV in one paragraph

**MERV** (Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value) measures how well a filter captures particles between 0.3 and 10 microns. Higher MERV catches more pollen, mold spores, and fine dust — but also **restricts airflow** if your system was not designed for it.

## Quick picker

| Your situation | Start here | Watch for |
|----------------|------------|-----------|
| Basic dust, no allergies | **MERV 8–10** | Cheap fiberglass — replace monthly, false economy |
| Kids with allergies, 1–2 pets | **MERV 11** | Replace on pressure, not marketing email |
| Asthma / severe pollen | **MERV 13** | Blower noise, longer cycles — check static pressure |
| Rental, window unit only | **Portable HEPA** | MERV on central system may not apply |

## MERV 11 vs MERV 13

**MERV 11** is the sweet spot for many single-stage furnaces: strong pollen and pet dander capture without crushing airflow on a 1" slot.

**MERV 13** adds finer particle capture. Worth it when:

- You have diagnosed respiratory triggers **and**
- Your HVAC tech confirmed the blower can handle the resistance **or**
- You use a 4" media cabinet designed for high-MERV media.

Not worth it when:

- The filter bows inward after 30 days (pressure warning).
- The furnace short-cycles or whistles after upgrade.

Read our hands-on comparison: [MERV 11 vs MERV 13 for allergies](/articles/merv-11-vs-13-allergies).

## Brand names vs MERV (Filtrete MPR, 3M, etc.)

Retail packaging uses **MPR** and marketing tiers. Always find the **printed MERV** on the frame or spec sheet before buying. A "Allergen Defense" label without MERV is a guess.

## Fridge and whole-house filters

Refrigerator filters are **not MERV-rated** — they are carbon + mechanical blocks for chlorine and particulates. Whole-house cartridges use vendor-specific micron ratings; treat them like HVAC media and log **pressure drop** at the housing gauge if you have one.
