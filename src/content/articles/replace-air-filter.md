Replacing your air filter regularly is one of the simplest yet most effective ways to maintain good indoor air quality and keep your HVAC system running efficiently. A clogged or dirty filter can restrict airflow, reduce system performance, and even lead to higher energy bills. But how do you know when it's time for a replacement?

Most experts recommend checking your air filter every 30 days and replacing it every 90 days, but this can vary depending on factors like pets, allergies, or local air quality. If you notice more dust around your home, reduced airflow from vents, or unusual HVAC noises, it might be time for a new filter. Let’s explore what to look for in a replacement and some of the best options available.

## What to Look For in an Air Filter

When shopping for a replacement air filter, consider the MERV rating (Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value), which indicates how effectively the filter captures particles. Filters with a MERV rating of 8-12 strike a good balance between filtration and airflow for most homes. Also, ensure the filter size matches your HVAC system—common sizes include 16x20x1 or 20x25x1. Pleated filters generally offer better performance than fiberglass ones, capturing more dust and allergens.

## How to Choose the Right Air Filter for Your Home

Your choice depends on your specific needs. If you have pets or allergies, opt for a higher MERV rating (12-13). For general use, a MERV 8-11 filter is sufficient. Always verify your HVAC system’s compatibility—some systems can’t handle high-MERV filters due to airflow restrictions. If you’re unsure, consult your HVAC manual or a professional.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I replace my air filter?**  
A: Every 90 days is standard, but check monthly and replace sooner if it looks dirty or you have pets.

**Q: Can I wash and reuse my air filter?**  
A: Only if it’s specifically labeled as washable. Most disposable filters should be replaced.

**Q: Does a higher MERV rating always mean better filtration?**  
A: Not necessarily. Higher MERV filters capture smaller particles but may restrict airflow in some systems.
