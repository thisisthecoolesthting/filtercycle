# Understanding CADR Ratings: What They Mean for Air Purifier Performance  

When shopping for an air purifier, you’ll often encounter the term **CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate)**. This metric is crucial for evaluating how effectively an air purifier removes pollutants like dust, pollen, and smoke from your indoor air. But what exactly does CADR measure, and how should you use it to choose the right air purifier for your needs?  

## What Is CADR?  

CADR is a standardized rating developed by the **Association of Home Appliance Manufacturers (AHAM)** to measure an air purifier’s efficiency in removing three common airborne pollutants:  

- **Dust** (particles like pet dander and household debris)  
- **Pollen** (fine particles from plants and outdoor sources)  
- **Tobacco smoke** (very fine particles and volatile organic compounds)  

The higher the CADR number, the faster the purifier can filter these contaminants from the air. CADR is measured in **cubic feet per minute (CFM)**, representing the volume of clean air delivered per minute.  

## How CADR Affects Air Purifier Performance  

A higher CADR means:  

- **Faster purification** – The unit can cycle air more quickly, reducing pollutant levels in less time.  
- **Better coverage** – A purifier with a high CADR can handle larger rooms effectively.  
- **Improved efficiency** – It removes more particles per pass, reducing strain on the filter.  

However, CADR alone doesn’t tell the whole story. Other factors, such as **filter type (HEPA, activated carbon, etc.)**, **room size compatibility**, and **fan speed settings**, also influence performance.  

## CADR vs. Other Air Purifier Metrics  

### CADR vs. MERV Ratings  

While CADR measures how quickly an air purifier cleans the air, **MERV (Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value)** rates the effectiveness of HVAC and furnace filters in trapping particles.  

- **MERV 8-12**: Good for capturing dust and pollen (common in home HVAC systems).  
- **MERV 13+**: Captures smaller particles like smoke and bacteria (often used in hospitals).  

Unlike CADR, MERV doesn’t account for airflow rate—just filtration efficiency. A high-MERV filter in an HVAC system may restrict airflow if the system isn’t designed for it.  

### CADR vs. ACH (Air Changes per Hour)  

**ACH** measures how many times an air purifier can refresh the air in a room per hour. A higher ACH means better air turnover.  

- **2-4 ACH**: Suitable for general use.  
- **5+ ACH**: Recommended for allergy sufferers or high-pollution areas.  

To calculate the needed CADR for your room:  

```  
CADR = (Room Volume in cubic feet × Desired ACH) / 60  
```  

For example, a 300 sq. ft. room with 8-foot ceilings (2,400 cubic feet) needing 5 ACH would require:  

```  
(2,400 × 5) / 60 = 200 CFM CADR  
```  

## Types of Air Purifiers and Their CADR Performance  

Not all air purifiers are equal in CADR performance. Here’s how different technologies compare:  

### **HEPA Air Purifiers**  
- **Best for**: Dust, pollen, mold spores.  
- **Typical CADR**: 150-350 CFM (varies by model and fan speed).  
- **Limitations**: Less effective against odors and gases unless paired with activated carbon.  

### **Activated Carbon Purifiers**  
- **Best for**: Smoke, VOCs, and odors.  
- **Typical CADR**: Lower for particles but excels in gas removal.  
- **Limitations**: Needs frequent replacement to remain effective.  

### **Ionic & UV Air Purifiers**  
- **Best for**: Germs and bacteria (UV) or electrostatic particle capture (ionic).  
- **Typical CADR**: Often lower than HEPA; some produce ozone (a lung irritant).  
- **Limitations**: Not ideal for large particles like pollen.  

## What to Look for in an Air Purifier Based on CADR  

When comparing air purifiers, consider:  

1. **Room Size Compatibility** – Match the CADR to your room’s square footage (AHAM recommends at least **2/3 of the room’s area in CADR**).  
   - Example: A 300 sq. ft. room needs a **200 CFM CADR** purifier.  

2. **Pollutant-Specific CADR** – Some purifiers perform better against smoke (fine particles) than pollen (larger particles). Check individual scores.  

3. **Noise Levels** – Higher CADR often means louder operation at max speed. Look for adjustable fan settings.  

4. **Filter Replacement Costs** – A high-CADR purifier with expensive filters may not be cost-effective long-term.  

5. **Energy Efficiency** – Check the **Energy Star** rating if electricity usage is a concern.  

## Conclusion  

CADR is a vital metric for evaluating air purifier performance, but it’s not the only factor. A unit with a high CADR for pollen may not excel at removing smoke, and vice versa. Pair CADR with considerations like **filter type, room size, and noise levels** to find the best air purifier for your needs.  

By understanding CADR and how it interacts with other air quality metrics, you can make an informed choice—ensuring cleaner, healthier air in your home.
