## Why Air Filtration Should Be Part of Your Home Renovation  

When planning a home renovation, air filtration is often overlooked—yet it’s one of the most impactful upgrades for indoor air quality. Whether you’re remodeling a single room or overhauling your entire HVAC system, integrating proper air filtration can reduce allergens, dust, and pollutants while improving overall comfort.  

This guide covers key strategies for incorporating air filtration into your renovation, from selecting the right HVAC filters to adding standalone air purifiers and improving ventilation.  

## Assessing Your Home’s Air Filtration Needs  

Before making changes, evaluate your home’s current air quality and filtration setup:  

- **Existing HVAC System:** Check if your furnace or central air system uses a standard filter slot. Most systems accommodate 1-inch to 4-inch filters, with thicker filters (like 4-inch MERV 11-13) offering better efficiency.  
- **Air Quality Concerns:** Identify specific issues—dust, pet dander, mold, or smoke—to determine the level of filtration needed.  
- **Ventilation:** Older homes may lack proper airflow, requiring mechanical ventilation (like an ERV or HRV) alongside filtration.  

## Upgrading Your HVAC Air Filters  

Your HVAC system is the backbone of whole-home air filtration. During a renovation, consider these upgrades:  

### 1. **Switch to a Higher-MERV Filter**  
   - **MERV 8-11:** Ideal for most homes, capturing dust, pollen, and mold spores.  
   - **MERV 13+:** Best for allergy sufferers or homes with pets, trapping smaller particles like bacteria and smoke.  
   - **Check Compatibility:** High-MERV filters restrict airflow if your system isn’t designed for them. Consult an HVAC professional before upgrading.  

### 2. **Install a Thicker Filter Housing**  
   - If renovating your HVAC system, opt for a 4- or 5-inch filter cabinet. Thicker filters last longer and provide better filtration without impeding airflow.  

### 3. **Add a Whole-Home Air Purifier**  
   - **UV-C Lights:** Installed in ducts, they kill mold and bacteria.  
   - **Electronic Air Cleaners:** Use electrostatic charges to trap particles, often paired with a MERV filter.  

## Standalone Air Purifiers for Targeted Filtration  

For rooms where HVAC filtration isn’t enough (e.g., bedrooms, home offices), consider portable air purifiers:  

- **HEPA Purifiers:** Remove 99.97% of particles as small as 0.3 microns, ideal for allergies.  
- **Activated Carbon Filters:** Absorb odors and VOCs, useful in kitchens or near garages.  
- **Smart Purifiers:** Monitor air quality in real-time and adjust fan speeds automatically.  

Place purifiers in high-traffic areas or near pollution sources (e.g., litter boxes, cooking areas).  

## Improving Ventilation for Cleaner Air  

Filtration works best with proper ventilation. During renovations:  

- **Add Exhaust Fans:** Install in kitchens and bathrooms to remove humidity and odors.  
- **Consider an ERV/HRV:** Energy Recovery Ventilators (ERVs) and Heat Recovery Ventilators (HRVs) bring in fresh air while retaining temperature control.  
- **Seal Ductwork:** Leaky ducts pull in dust and reduce efficiency. Have them inspected and sealed if accessible during renovations.  

## Sealing Gaps to Reduce Pollutants  

Air filtration is less effective if unfiltered air leaks in. As part of your renovation:  

- **Weatherstrip Doors/Windows:** Prevents outdoor allergens from entering.  
- **Insulate Attics and Crawl Spaces:** Reduces dust and mold infiltration.  
- **Use Low-VOC Materials:** Choose paints, adhesives, and flooring that emit fewer pollutants.  

## What to Look for in Air Filtration Products  

When selecting filters or purifiers, prioritize:  

- **MERV Rating:** Match the filter to your HVAC system’s capacity (MERV 8-13 for most homes).  
- **Filter Longevity:** Thicker filters (4-inch) last 6-12 months; 1-inch filters need replacement every 1-3 months.  
- **CADR (for Purifiers):** Higher Clean Air Delivery Rate means faster filtration.  
- **Noise Levels:** Purifiers should operate quietly, especially in bedrooms.  

## Final Tips for a Successful Renovation  

- **Consult an HVAC Pro:** Ensure your system can handle upgrades like high-MERV filters or UV lights.  
- **Layer Solutions:** Combine HVAC filters, purifiers, and ventilation for the best results.  
- **Plan for Maintenance:** Factor in filter replacement costs and schedules.  

By integrating air filtration into your renovation, you’ll create a healthier, more comfortable living space for years to come.
